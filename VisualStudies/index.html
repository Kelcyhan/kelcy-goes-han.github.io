01 / Repetition & contrast

Repetition &
contrast.

Repeated lines establish rhythm; shifts in scale suggest depth. Black and white compositions explore the contrast between filled and open areas, curves and diagonals.

The next group carries repetition into image layering and symmetrical pattern.

02 / Shape & pattern

Shape, layers
& symmetry.

A silhouette contains an image; repeated flowers form a layered composition; mirrored colour shapes build a symmetrical pattern. Each exercise explores a different way to organise visual material.

03 / Colour & tone

Colour & tone.
One scene, four palettes.

With the composition held constant, four palettes explore how hue, tonal contrast and saturation change the scene. Compare the colour relationships without changing the subject.

04 / Texture & distortion

Surface, movement
& transformation.

These image-treatment exercises explore a spectrum of colour and surface effects: swirling direction, dense layered texture and radiating forms. The emphasis is on how treatment changes the reading of an image.

Image-treatment exercises. Original source material and editing steps are not yet documented.

05 / Mosaic & collage

Fragment
& recombine.

Mosaic treatment breaks an image into smaller units of colour. Collage combines existing imagery into a new arrangement. These two exercises focus on processing and recomposition.

Exercises using existing image material. The street photograph and artwork reproductions are not presented as original photography or painting; source files and reference credits remain to be confirmed.

From technique to application / New concept · 2026

Meet OFF HOURS.

A fictional coffee & play identity extending the colour exercise. The yellow illustration becomes the starting point for a shared palette, a clear message and two layouts in the same visual family.

Concept proposal, with AI-assisted copy, layout and code. Original artwork is retained; this is not a client campaign or a claim of commercial results.

From colour exercise to communication

Same ingredients.
A different arrangement.

  1. Separate image and message.

    The complete illustration has its own square. Type sits on a quiet cream field, so no headline competes with the bowling pins or coffee cup.

  2. Set a reading order.

    Identity → headline → supporting line → invitation. An expressive serif carries the headline; a simple sans serif handles smaller information.

  3. Recompose for each format.

    The portrait puts the headline above the image and a short text column beside it. The banner places the message on the left and the image on the right, with shorter copy.
