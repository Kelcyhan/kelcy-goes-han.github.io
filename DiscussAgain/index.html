The problem

One discussion. One chance.

Group discussions are how we rehearse social-interaction competence. Yet they evaporate the moment they end — no rewind, no second take, no targeted retry.

Especially painful in language learning and educational settings, where a quiet learner often only realises what they wanted to say five minutes later.

Why this matters

"I finally understood what I should have said — five minutes after we'd already moved on."

  • Group conversation = the primary site of interaction-competence learning
  • One-off discussions can't be debugged, replayed, or targeted
  • Language learners + quiet students lose disproportionately

The pipeline

From transcript to replayable agents.

A four-stage pipeline: capture → speaker-attribute → persona-distill → multi-agent re-enact. Each participant becomes a steered LLM agent with memory of the original arc.

Designed so you can drop in, pause anyone, hijack the floor, and watch the conversation re-route around your new contribution.

System & framework

Steering the replay.

Floor manager, turn-taking model, persona memory, and a competence-tracking dashboard — composed into a real-time agent orchestration runtime.

Built with FastAPI + WebSocket backend; React frontend; agents driven by GPT-class models with custom turn-taking heuristics.

Walk-through

Findings

What replay unlocks.

Across a user study, participants reported greater willingness to take risks knowing the conversation could be rewound — and better recall of competence-relevant moments.

Quantitative: significant improvement on self-rated readiness for similar future discussions. Qualitative: "I finally understood what I should have said."

Reflection

Practice rooms, not transcripts.

Multi-agent systems make a new kind of practice room possible — one where your past conversations can be re-entered, not just reviewed. Submitted to UIST 2026.

My contribution: interaction design for the floor-control UI, the post-replay dashboard, and the full design system bridging research paper figures and the deployed prototype.
