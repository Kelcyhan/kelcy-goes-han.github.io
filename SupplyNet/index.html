SupplyNet

Case Study · Research

A visual exploratory-learning environment for supply-chain dynamics — students step into a contextual multi-agent simulation and steer the system from any seat in the chain.

Context

Supply chains, made tangible.

Supply-chain education leans on the HBS Root Beer Game — a powerful but rigid metaphor. We wanted a system where uncertainty, propagation, and trade-offs can be felt, not just lectured.

Formative study with 7 students and 3 instructors surfaced design principles around exploration, role-switching, and visible causal chains.

Formative study

"I want to see what happens if I just stop ordering for a week — but the textbook only lets me read about it."

  • 7 students + 3 instructors, semi-structured interviews
  • Root Beer Game perceived as effective but rigid
  • Lacks causal propagation visibility
  • No room for exploratory "what if" play

System overview

A multi-agent chain you can steer.

SupplyNet stages five linked agents — raw material, manufacturer, distributor, wholesaler, retailer — each driven by an LLM with role-specific goals and memory.

The player can occupy any node, change strategy mid-run, and watch causal effects ripple outward.

Onboarding

Pick your seat at the table.

The onboarding flow walks new students through choosing a node, setting their initial strategy, and meeting the agents they'll be coordinating with.

Designed to surface the agency-vs-uncertainty tension before the first decision is even made.

Analysis consoles

Three lenses, one reality.

Financials, Logistics, Operations — three coordinated consoles let players hypothesise, intervene, and read consequences across timescales.

Each console shares state but tells a different story; designed so the same disruption reads differently as a cost spike, a stock crash, or a routing snarl.

Findings

Active reflection, not passive lecture.

14 postgraduate and undergraduate students used SupplyNet over 90 minutes. Participants reported greater engagement and richer reflective practice than the baseline Root Beer Game.

"It made me think about the chain as a relationship, not a flow."

Study results

"I had to actually keep checking causal propagation — it forced me to think across the chain."
