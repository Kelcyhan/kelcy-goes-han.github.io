Product features

Designed around the room.

The product is not just a screen flow. It depends on classroom-aware sensing: a panoramic camera captures the group context, while offline speaker diarization separates who spoke when without relying on cloud audio processing.

Panoramic camera

A shared 360-degree capture point keeps every candidate visible, reducing setup anxiety and making group speaking behavior easier to review.

Local diarization separates speakers and timestamps turns in the room, supporting privacy-sensitive assessment and faster feedback generation.

The actual designed flow.

These screenshots are cropped from the Figma case-study node you linked. They show the intended desktop flow: onboarding, task entry, system checks, group login, camera setup, identity confirmation, topic selection, recording, and report generation.

Identity + voice binding

Make recognition observable.

The Figma flow exposes recognition status instead of hiding it: candidate tags, camera confirmation, voice sample, and retry states are all visible to the room.

Topic · record · report

The session becomes trackable.

The later Figma screens cue the final classroom sequence: topic detection, preparation timer, active recording state, report generation, and QR handoff for personal feedback.

Mobile feedback

Personal coaching on the student's phone.

The feedback flow moves from classroom-level reporting to individual mobile guidance: students bind their phone, choose focus areas, wait for processing, then review metrics, timeline evidence, and targeted improvement suggestions.
